# Authentic Awadhi Grammar Sources

To ensure our phonetic and grammatical "Gotcha" boxes (which explain anomalies like 17-beat oral compressions, `mh` weights, and repha dropping) are backed by rigorous linguistic scholarship, we should cite the following authoritative academic sources on Tulsidas's Awadhi in our Appendix:

## 1. The Foundational Text
**"Evolution of Awadhi (a Branch of Hindi)" by Dr. Baburam Saksena (1937)**
* **Why it's essential:** This is universally considered the seminal, foundational academic work on the Awadhi language. It meticulously documents the phonetic notation, historical stages, and exact syntactical rules of early Awadhi.

## 2. IIT Kanpur Ramcharitmanas Portal
**"https://www.ramcharitmanas.iitk.ac.in/content/tulsidas"**
* **Why it's essential:** The definitive digital authority and academic portal for Tulsidas's epic, providing rigorous, accessible, and highly accurate digitized references for his specific dialects and literary framework.

## 3. The Tulsidas-Specific Grammar Guide
**"Reading the Rāmcaritmānas: A Companion to the Awadhi Text of Tulsīdās" by Rupert Snell and Neha Tiwari**
* **Why it's essential:** Rupert Snell is one of the world's leading scholars of pre-modern Hindi dialects. This unpacks the exact grammatical quirks, postposition drops, and metric compressions Tulsidas employs.

## 4. The Classic Linguistic Survey
**"Linguistic Survey of India" (Vol. VI: Indo-Aryan Family) by George Abraham Grierson (1904)**
* **Why it's essential:** Grierson's monumental survey was the first to formally classify Awadhi as "Eastern Hindi" and document its unique phonological rules.
