Glossary of Divine Terms

Deep structural definitions of theological lists compiled in the Chalisa.

The Eight Siddhis (Ashta-Siddhi)

In the 31st Chaupai, Mother Sita blesses Hanuman with the ability to grant the *Ashta-Siddhi* (the eight supernatural Yogic powers). A master of these Siddhis can manipulate the fundamental building blocks of matter and space.

Anima अणिमा

The power to shrink one's body to the size of an atom or sub-atomic particle (used to secretly enter Lanka).

Mahima महिमा

The power to expand one's body to an infinitely large, gargantuan size (used to terrify the demoness Surasa).

Garima गरिमा

The power to become infinitely heavy (used when Bhima could not lift even Hanuman's tail).

Laghima लघिमा

The power to become almost weightless or lighter than air (used for levitation and swift flight across the ocean).

Prapti प्राप्ति

The power to instantly acquire or reach anything anywhere, regardless of physical barriers.

Prakamya प्राकाम्य

The power to realize any desire, achieve irresistible willpower, and instantly materialize objects.

Ishitva ईशित्व

The power of absolute lordship and supreme dominance over nature and living beings.

Vashitva वशित्व

The ultimate power to conquer, subjugate, or mesmerize any entity to follow one's will.

The Nine Nidhis (Nava-Nidhi)

Alongside the 8 Siddhis, Sita also granted Hanuman dominion over the *Nava-Nidhi* (the nine divine, inexhaustible treasures of Kubera, the god of wealth). These are not merely financial riches, but profound spiritual and cosmic abundances.

Mahapadma महापद्म

The ''Great Lotus Casket'' containing immense wealth, generating generations of prosperity and philanthropy.

Padma पद्म

The ''Lotus Casket'' offering abundant material riches, sattvic tendencies, and untainted gifts.

Shankha शङ्ख

The ''Conch Shell'' bringing self-sustaining wealth, typically enjoyed and isolated by the owner.

Makara मकर

The ''Crocodile'' granting martial proficiency, strategic intellect, and dominance in warfare/arms.

Kacchapa कच्छप

The ''Tortoise'' establishing wealth accumulated slowly, hidden secretly, and passed down carefully.

Mukunda मुकुन्द

The ''Quicksilver'' or ''Kettle'' bringing an aesthetic inclination towards fine arts, music, and royal patronage.

Kunda कुन्द

The ''Jasmine'' representing wealth related to morality, spiritual upliftment, and charitable endowments.

Nila नील

The ''Sapphire'' giving massive resources related to trade, commerce, and material exchange.

Kharva खर्व

The ''Dwarf'' representing wealth intertwined with mixed (both auspicious and complex) desires.

The Four Yugas (Chatur-Yuga)

In the 29th Chaupai, Tulsidas proclaims that Hanuman's glory is famous across all four ages (*Yugas*). According to Vedic cosmology, time is cyclical, divided into four grand planetary epochs.

Satya Yuga सत्ययुग

The Age of Truth and absolute Purity. Humanity is deeply spiritual, peaceful, and perfectly completely aligned with Dharma.

Treta Yuga त्रेतायुग

The Silver Age. Dharma slightly declines. This is the era in which Lord Rama and Hanuman incarnated to establish righteousness.

Dvapara Yuga द्वापरयुग

The Bronze Age. Morality drops to 50\%. This era featured the Mahabharata war and the incarnation of Lord Krishna.

Kali Yuga कलियुग

The Iron Age (our current era). An age of deep ignorance, conflict, and spiritual amnesia. Hanuman remains immortal (*Chiranjeevi*) physically present on Earth specifically to guide seekers through this dark age.

The Four Fruits of Life (Phala Chaari)

In the very first opening Doha, Tulsidas prays for the ''four fruits'' (*Phala Chaari*). In Vedic philosophy, these are the *Purusharthas*—the four fundamental grand goals or aims of human life that lead to complete fulfillment.

Dharma धर्म

Righteousness, moral duty, and living in cosmic law and harmony. The foundational aim of a noble life.

Artha अर्थ

Material prosperity, wealth, economic security, and physical well-being required to sustain life and family.

Kama काम

Desire, pleasure, emotional fulfillment, aesthetics, and the enjoyment of the world in a balanced way.

Moksha मोक्ष

The ultimate liberation. Freedom from the cycle of birth and death (*Samsara*) and union with the divine.
