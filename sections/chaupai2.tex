\vspace{0pt}
\begin{tcolorbox}[anchorbox]
\textbf{The Ultimate Reliable Executive:} He is called the ultimate Messenger (\textit{Rama Duta}). When millions of soldiers were dispatched to find Sita, Lord Rama handed his personal signet ring to Hanuman alone. Leadership means identifying and encouraging people to be the best they can be and capable of taking on hard challenges.  Hanuman inspires us to be our best by example, as he was relied upon to execute the hardest mission of reaching Lanka and finding Sita.
\vspace{0.5em}\newline Out of millions, Lord Rama entrusted his signet ring uniquely to Hanuman because of his unmatched reliability.\newline\null\hfill\href{https://www.valmiki.iitk.ac.in/sloka?field_kanda_tid=4&language=dv&field_sarga_value=44}{\textit{Kishkindha Kanda, 44:12-14}}
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{minipage}

    \textbf{ \deva{सरल-संस्कृतम्}:}\\
 \deva{हे रामदूत! त्वम् अतुलित-बलस्य धाम (आश्रयः) असि।}\\
 \deva{हे अञ्जनिपुत्र! त्वं पवनसुत-नाम्ना प्रसिद्धः असि॥}\\

O Emissary of Rama! You are the abode of incomparable strength.\\
O Son of Anjani! You are famous by the name 'Son of the Wind'.

\begin{tcolorbox}[poetrybox]
\textbf{Praasa (The Rhyme Engine):}\\
Note the elongated \deva{आ} (aa) at the end of \deva{धामा} (\textit{dhāmā}) and \deva{नामा} (\textit{nāmā}). Sanskrit/Hindi would use \deva{धाम} (Dhaam) and \deva{नाम} (Naam), but Awadhi uses the heavy 2-beat suffix to complete the 16-beat line and create the musical bounce.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[gotchabox]
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Conjunct Consonant Weight (\deva{पुत्र}):} The short 'u' in \deva{पु} gets weighed down and becomes a 2-beat Guru because it crashes into the conjunct consonant \deva{त्र} (t+ra).
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
