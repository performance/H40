चतुर्दश चौपाई (Fourteenth Chaupai)

सनकादिक ब्रह्मादि मुनीसा।
नारद सारद सहित अहीसा॥
సనకాదిక బ్రహ్మాది మునీసా।
నారద సారద సహిత అహీసా॥
sanakādika brahmādi munīsā |
nārada sārada sahita ahīsā ||
सरल-संस्कृतम्:
सनकादयः (सनक-सनन्दन-सनातन-सनत्कुमाराः), ब्रह्मा-आदयः देवाः, मुनीशाः च
नारदः, देवी सरस्वती (शारदा), शेषनाग-सहिताः (अहीशः) अपि यस्य यशः सम्यक् वर्णयितुं न शक्नुवन्ति।
Sages like Sanaka, gods like Brahma, and great ascetics
even Narada, Saraswati, and the Lord of Serpents.

The Universal Scholar

Sages, gods, and cosmic figures (Sanaka, Brahma, Narada, Sarasvati, and Shesha) praise Hanuman's intellectual greatness. In the *Valmiki Ramayana* (*Kishkindha Kanda, 3:28-32*), Rama marvels at Hanuman's flawless speech and mastery of the Vedas and grammar, stating that even a king with such a brilliant advisor/envoy succeeds in all his endeavors.

💡

Linguistic Notes

  • The श/ष → स Shift in Action: Beautiful symmetry here! Sanskrit मुनीश (Muneesha), शारदा (Shaarada), and अहीश (Aheesha) drop their heavy palatal श (sh) and soften into the pure Awadhi rhymes: मुनीसा (Muneesaa), सारद (Saarad), and अहीसा (Aheesaa).
  • The 'Aadi' (आदि) suffix: The suffix आदि (ādi) is used to denote "and others like them" or "starting from". This chaupai starts a list of greats, but we will know about why they are mentioned here in the next chaupai.
