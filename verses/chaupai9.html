नवम चौपाई (Ninth Chaupai)

सूक्ष्म रूप धरि सियहिं दिखावा।
बिकट रूप धरि लंक जरावा॥
సూక్ష్మ రూప ధరి సియహిఁ దిఖావా।
బికట రూప ధరి లంక జరావా॥
sūkṣma rūpa dhari siyahĩ dikhāvā |
bikaṭa rūpa dhari laṅka jarāvā ||
सरल-संस्कृतम्:
भवान् सूक्ष्मं (लघु) रूपं धृत्वा सीतायै (सियहिं) दर्शनं दत्तवान्।
भवान् विकटं (भयानकं/विशालं) रूपं धृत्वा लङ्कां भस्मीकृतवान् (जरावा)।
Assuming a microscopic form, you revealed yourself to Mother Sita.
Assuming a massive/terrifying form, you burned down Lanka.

Emotional Intelligence and Scale

Hanuman knew exactly how to scale his approach. He approached allies in a tiny, unthreatening form (Sukshma Rupa) to build trust and offer comfort, but assumed a massive, formidable form (Bikata Rupa) when he needed to defend himself. Emotional intelligence requires adapting your approach to match the needs of the situation.

💡

Linguistic Notes

  • The Dative Suffix (सियहिं): In Awadhi, the formal name सीता softens to सिय (Siya). The -हिं (-hiṃ) is the objective marker we saw earlier in मोहिं (to me). So सियहिं means "To Mother Siya".
  • Phonetic Shifts: Sanskrit विकट (Vikata) becomes बिकट (Bikata) via the v → b shift. Also, the causative verb जरावा (Jaraavaa) comes from the root ज्वल् (to burn), meaning "caused to burn".
